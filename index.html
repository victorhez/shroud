    01

    Get a wallet that does the hard part for you

    A Zcash wallet holds the keys that control your coins. A good one speaks shielded by default, so privacy is the path of least resistance rather than a setting you hunt for.

    Look for Unified Address supportLook for Orchard supportOpen source

    Reference and community wallets worth looking at include Zodl, Zingo and YWallet. Shroud does not endorse any product: check each project’s official site and install only from the official app store or release page.

    Never, ever

    Your recovery phrase is the wallet. Nobody legitimate will ask for it: not support, not an “airdrop”, not this website. Shroud’s Inspector actively refuses to read it.

    02

    Get a little ZEC

    You only need a small amount to learn on. Fees are tiny: a typical shielded send costs around 0.0001 ZEC under ZIP-317, so a fraction of one coin is plenty.

    You can buy on an exchange that lists ZEC, or swap in from another chain with a cross-chain service such as NEAR Intents. Whichever route you take, ask for the withdrawal to go to your wallet’s unified or shielded address if the sender supports it. Many don’t yet, and then it arrives at a transparent address, which is exactly what chapter 4 is for.

    Reality check

    Buying from a regulated exchange ties the purchase to your identity at that exchange. Zcash can’t change what a third party already knows. It protects what happens after the coins reach your own shielded wallet.

    03

    Read an address like a pro

    Zcash has two worlds. Transparent addresses (starting t1 / t3) work like Bitcoin: public to everyone. Shielded pools (Sapling, then the newer Orchard) hide sender, receiver and amount behind zero-knowledge proofs.

    A Unified Address (starts u1) bundles several receivers into one string. The sender’s wallet picks the best one it understands, ideally Orchard. Paste yours into the Inspector and watch it get taken apart in your browser, byte by byte.

    Shield: step across the line

    If your ZEC landed at a transparent address, shielding moves it into your shielded balance. Your wallet does it in one tap, usually labelled “Shield”.

    Shielding itself is visible: the chain sees that value entered the pool and how much. What it can’t see is anything you do afterwards. That is why the next two chapters matter.

    05

    Send and receive in private

    A shielded-to-shielded payment reveals nothing public beyond the fact that a transaction occurred. You can attach an encrypted memo (up to 512 bytes) that only the recipient can read, great for invoices and notes.

    To ask someone to pay you, build a payment request (ZIP-321): a link and QR code carrying address, amount and memo, so nobody mistypes anything.

    06

    Unshield, or better, don’t

    Sometimes you must cash out to a transparent address. Unshielding is the other public step: the amount and the destination become visible. The risk isn’t the step itself. It’s pattern matching: the same amount, soon after, to the same address.

    Questions

    Is shielded ZEC really private?

    Shielded transactions hide sender, recipient and amount using zero-knowledge proofs. The risks are at the edges: transparent entry and exit points, reused addresses, and metadata outside the chain. That is exactly what the Leak Lab exists to catch.

    What’s the difference between Sapling and Orchard?

    Both are shielded pools. Orchard (2022) uses the Halo 2 proving system, which needs no trusted setup, and is the recommended default. Moving value between pools reveals the amount that crossed, so it’s best to stay in one.

    Why does shielding show up on-chain?

    The transaction has to take transparent inputs, so their address and total are public. Inside the pool, nothing is. Shielding is a one-way doorway: public to the door, private after it.

    Does Shroud ever see my address?

    No. The Inspector and Request Builder run in your browser and never send what you type anywhere. Nothing is stored except your course checkboxes, in local storage.

    How accurate is the Inspector?

    It performs a full ZIP-316 decode: Bech32m checksum, F4Jumble inversion, HRP padding check, and typecode parsing. The test suite replays official vectors from the zcash-test-vectors repository.
